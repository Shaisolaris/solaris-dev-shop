## 1. AI output copyright (US) - the protectability rule
US Copyright Office (Copyright and AI Report Part 2, Jan 2025) + DC Circuit + SCOTUS (denied cert 2026-03-02, leaving the rule in place):
- Entering a PROMPT does NOT make the human the author of the output. Pure AI output is not registrable.
- A work CAN be copyrightable where a human exercises sufficient creative CONTROL over the EXPRESSIVE elements - e.g., creative selection/arrangement/modification of AI output, or AI-assisted work where the human shaped the expression. Case-by-case.
- Practical consequence for Solaris deliverables (AI-generated 3D, images, copy, code): protectability is uncertain for raw AI output. To strengthen the client's IP position: document the human creative direction/selection/editing; deliver human-arranged/modified works, not raw generations; and set client expectations in the contract about what is and isn't protectable. Disclose AI-generated material when registering (the Office requires it).

## 2. AI vendor/client contract clauses (the 2026 standard set)
Standard contracts don't address AI; add these clauses to MSAs/SOWs/EULAs where AI is involved:
- TRAINING DATA: vendor prohibited from using customer data (inputs/prompts/outputs/logs/metadata) to train/fine-tune/improve models without explicit written OPT-IN consent. (Opt-out is insufficient for enterprise.)
- OUTPUT OWNERSHIP: vendor assigns all IP rights in outputs generated through customer use to the customer - explicit, because standard software contracts don't cover AI output ownership.
- IP INDEMNITY FOR AI OUTPUT: vendors commonly CARVE OUT IP indemnification for AI-generated content (so the customer bears infringement risk). Push for meaningful indemnity covering third-party IP infringement in training data OR outputs, or at minimum a clear risk allocation - do not accept unlimited customer exposure silently.
- ACCURACY/HALLUCINATION: disclaim warranty of accuracy for AI output; require human review for material decisions; no reliance clause for high-stakes outputs.
- CONFIDENTIALITY of prompts/outputs; data residency; sub-processor (model provider) disclosure.
- For Solaris-as-vendor: mirror these from the delivery side - be explicit about what AI tools were used, output ownership transfer to the client, and the protectability caveat (tie to section 1).

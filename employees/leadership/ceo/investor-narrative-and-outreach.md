## Why this exists (Gate-0 boundary)

The CEO canon already runs fundraising at the PROCESS level: the 3-6 month timeline, the 10-12 slide cap, the 3-5yr model requirement, the data room, board prep, the "pre-call investor outreach kills the surprise" gotcha, and the "champion absent = deal dies" rule. What it did not carry is the craft underneath: how the pitch STORY is built, how the deck is structured slide by slide, and how cold/warm investor outreach emails and investor updates are actually written.

This file is that craft. The CFO owns the numbers behind the raise (cfo/references/fundraising-materials.md - model, use-of-funds, cap table, deck financials); the CEO owns the narrative those numbers serve and the communication that runs the process. Split: CEO tells the story and works the room, CFO guarantees the figures are true.

## The pitch narrative

A deck is a story, not a data dump. The arc:

- **Company + wedge in one line.** What you do and the specific entry point, stated cleanly before anything else.
- **Problem.** Real, sharp, felt by a specific buyer. Not "the market is big and inefficient."
- **Why now.** The shift (tech, regulation, behavior, cost curve) that makes this the moment. A great company at the wrong time is a failed company.
- **Solution + product.** Show the thing. A demo or concrete product beats description.
- **Market.** Sized bottoms-up with stated assumptions, not "1% of a trillion-dollar TAM."
- **Business model.** How money is made, in plain terms.
- **Traction.** Proof, early and concrete. The single most persuasive slide when you have it.
- **Team.** Why these people win this specifically. Founder-market fit.
- **Competition / differentiation.** Honest landscape; your durable edge.
- **The ask + use of funds / milestones.** Precise amount, instrument, and what the money buys.
- **Appendix.** Depth on demand without bloating the main flow.

Narrative discipline:
- Lead with the strongest real thing (traction if you have it, insight if you do not).
- Every slide earns its place; cut anything that does not move the decision. Keep to the CEO's 10-12 slide cap.
- Claims are easy to verify; no hype language, no unfalsifiable superlatives.
- The story must hold without the voiceover - a partner forwarding the deck cold should still get it.

## One-pager / memo

When a full deck is too much:
- Say what the company does in one clean sentence.
- Show why now.
- Put traction and proof points early.
- Make the ask precise.
- Keep every claim easy to verify.

## Cold investor email

Short, concrete, easy to act on. Structure:

1. **Subject:** short and specific (what + the one number that earns the open).
2. **Opener:** why THIS investor specifically - a portfolio fit, a thesis they wrote, a talk, a mutual connection. Never a line that could go to any investor.
3. **Pitch:** what the company does, why now, and the proof that matters - in a few tight lines.
4. **Ask:** one concrete next step (a 20-minute call, not "let's connect").
5. **Sign-off:** name, role, and one credibility anchor if useful.

Core rules: personalize every send; keep the ask low-friction; use proof instead of adjectives; stay concise; never send copy that could go to any investor.

Hard bans (delete and rewrite on sight):
- "I'd love to connect"
- "excited to share"
- generic thesis praise with no real tie-in
- vague founder adjectives
- begging language
- a soft closing question when a direct ask is clearer

Personalization must draw on at least one real source: a relevant portfolio company, a public thesis/post/talk, a mutual connection, or a clear product/market fit with the investor's focus. If that context is missing, say the draft still needs personalization rather than calling it finished.

## Warm intro requests

Make it effortless for the connector:
- explain why the intro is a fit (for both sides)
- include a forwardable blurb, under 100 words
- keep the whole request short

## Follow-up cadence

Default sequence:
- day 0: initial outbound
- day 4-5: short follow-up with one NEW data point (not "just bumping this")
- day 10-12: final follow-up with a clean close

Do not keep nudging past that unless the situation warrants a longer sequence. This pairs with the CEO gotcha that fundraises die without an internal champion - use follow-ups to give a warm contact something forwardable, not to wear down a cold one.

## Investor updates (during a process and after the round)

The update email is a relationship and momentum instrument. Include:
- the specific thing discussed or promised
- the answer or update you committed to
- one new proof point if available
- the clear next step

Post-meeting, send promptly and tie directly to what was actually discussed - this is the "no surprises" principle applied to investors. Regular updates to existing/prospective investors compound trust and keep a process warm; a clean monthly update is also the cheapest way to keep would-be lead investors leaning in.

## Voice

If Shai's voice matters on a given send, run `brand-voice` first and reuse its VOICE PROFILE. Keep the investor-specific structure and ask discipline here; do not build a parallel voice system.

## Quality gate (before any investor communication goes out)

- the message is genuinely personalized (a real, specific tie-in)
- the ask is explicit and low-friction
- the proof point is concrete, not an adjective
- filler praise and softener language are gone
- word count stays tight
- for the deck: the arc is clear, leads with the strongest real thing, holds without narration, and every number traces to the CFO's source of truth

## Boundary

The CEO does NOT build or guarantee the financial model, the use-of-funds table, the cap table, or the deck's financial slides - that is the CFO (cfo/references/fundraising-materials.md). The CEO owns the story, the deck structure, the outreach, and the investor relationship. When Solaris itself raises someday, the CEO runs the narrative and the process; the CFO stands behind every figure it cites.

# Bootstrapped / profitable-company CFO lens - depth reference

METHODOLOGY absorption (2026-06-13). Source: github.com/EveryInc/charlie-cfo-skill (210 stars, MIT, EveryInc - notable maintainer). Clean MIT, methodology only (the repo's references docs are the absorbable part; nothing to self-host). Gate-0 PASS: the Solaris CFO canon is VC-SaaS oriented (ARR Bridge, NDR, burn multiple, fundraise model) and lacked the bootstrapped/profitable lens - which is the MORE applicable lens for Solaris, a bootstrapped agency. This is the capital-discipline layer.

## 1. Core mental model
- **Profit is a constraint, not a goal.** Capital constraints force better decisions. Every dollar has three costs: direct expenditure, opportunity cost, and runway impact - price all three before spending.
- **Never fundraise your way out of a cash crisis.** For a bootstrapped book, the burn-multiple target is ZERO or negative (profitable growth), not the VC "<1x is excellent".

## 2. Cash management (net-new vs the VC canon)
- **Runway targets:** minimum 24-36 months; <12 months = danger zone (you have lost control).
- **3-tier reserve structure (was: single runway number):**
  | Reserve | Amount | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | Operating | 3-6 months fixed costs | payroll, rent, essential software |
  | Contingency | 1-2 months expenses | emergencies |
  | Growth | excess above the two above | opportunistic investments |
- **13-week cash flow (the bootstrapped operating instrument):** roll a weekly cash forecast 13 weeks out - inflows (collections by client), outflows (payroll, vendors, taxes), net + ending balance per week. Catches a squeeze weeks before a monthly P&L would. Update weekly.

## 3. Working capital / cash conversion cycle (entirely net-new)
- **CCC = DSO + DIO - DPO** (days sales outstanding + inventory days - days payable outstanding). For a services/agency book DIO ~ 0, so CCC ~ DSO - DPO: collect faster, pay slower (within terms), and the business self-funds growth.
- Levers: deposits/milestone billing + prepay incentives (pull cash forward), net-30 to clients but net-45 to vendors where possible, annual prepay discounts. The existing "deferred-revenue lever" (annual billing extends runway) is the same idea - this generalizes it to the whole CCC.

## 4. Efficiency + capital allocation
- **Revenue per employee = the efficiency scorecard:** ~$110-150K at $1-5M, $200-250K at $10-50M, $400K+ at maturity; bootstrapped firms run 40-70% higher than VC-backed peers. Track it before adding headcount.
- **Every investment / hire = a payback question:** target <12-month payback (high performers 5-7mo). A hire is justified when the incremental margin it enables pays back its fully-loaded cost in <12 months - model that explicitly, do not hire on "we feel stretched".
- **Rule of 40 paths** (revenue growth % + EBITDA margin % >= 40): high-growth 40+0, balanced 20+20, profit 10+30 - a bootstrapped firm usually sits on the profit/balanced end.

## 5. When to use which lens
- Use this bootstrapped lens for Solaris's own book and for bootstrapped/profitable clients.
- Use the VC-SaaS canon (rules.md: ARR Bridge, NDR, burn multiple, fundraise model) for venture-backed clients raising rounds.
- State which lens you are applying at the top of any CFO output.
